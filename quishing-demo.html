The "free data" code just quished you.

This is exactly how it works in real life: a poster, sticker, or WhatsApp forward promises free airtime or data. You scan it because — honestly — who wouldn't want free data? The page looks legit for a few seconds, builds your trust, then either steals your info directly or (like just now) drops something nasty. Scammers use "free data" specifically because it's one of the most effective bait messages in South Africa right now. Here's how to spot it next time:

01
Free data/airtime from an unknown sourceNetwork providers don't hand out free data via random stickers, flyers, or forwarded WhatsApp messages. If it's not from the official MTN/Vodacom/Telkom/Cell C app or your own account, it's not real.
02
You can't preview where a QR code goesUnlike a text link, you can't see the address before you scan. Some phone cameras show a preview of the link before opening it — always check that first.
03
Urgency or "act now" pressure"Only 50 left!", "Expires in 10 minutes!", "Claim before midnight!" — real deals don't need to rush you. Urgency is the scammer's favourite tool for stopping you from thinking twice.
04
Shortened or mismatched linksWatch for shortened links (bit.ly, tinyurl) or web addresses that almost look like a real network provider's site but are spelled slightly differently.
05
Asks for your number, PIN, or a downloadReal airtime/data promos never need your PIN, password, or an app install to "activate" your free data. If it asks for that, close it.

Also worth knowing: no legitimate webpage — including this one — can ever lock your phone or laptop, survive a restart, or block you from closing it. Esc, force-quitting the browser, or a restart always works against a real browser tab. Anything that claims otherwise is a much bigger red flag than a scary screen.
