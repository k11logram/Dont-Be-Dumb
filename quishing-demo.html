QR code phishing works exactly like this: a QR code takes you somewhere that looks fun or harmless, builds trust for a few seconds, then delivers the real payload — a fake login page, a malicious download, or a script that runs automatically. Here's what to watch for in real life:

01
Unknown source, no contextA QR code on a random flyer, sticker, or parking meter with no clear owner is a red flag — legit ones usually come with a recognizable brand or person attached.
02
You can't preview the linkUnlike a text link, you can't hover over a QR code to see where it goes before you commit. Some camera apps show a URL preview before opening — always check it.
03
Urgency or reward bait"You won!", "Limited time!", "Verify now or lose access!" — urgency and rewards are classic pressure tactics to stop you from thinking twice.
04
Shortened or mismatched URLsWatch for shortened links (bit.ly, tinyurl) or domains that almost match a real brand name but aren't quite right.
05
Asks for login, payment, or a downloadReal prizes don't need your school password. If a scanned page asks you to log in or install something, stop.
